## Accepted behavior and scope

Before M-14, host/OS fields and existing project, store, deployment, package-lock and artifact records retained their legacy local meanings; there was no separate typed environment definition/executor/target/generation model or admitted bound deployment. A.1 introduced five separately owned, supplied identity roles and three machine roles without treating their references as authenticated content or physical authority. A.2 added injected layout/root, target-path, resource and prefix policy as data while preserving the actual settings, `bin`, `store`, `deployments` and project paths. Its [interface review](environment-layout-affected-interface-review.md) and [documentation disposition](environment-layout-documentation-review.md) limit the claim to pure values and equal-value aliases.

B.1 registered epoch-one environment value contracts and distinct exact-lock versus realized-generation digest recipes. Selected package/configuration/target facts and payload bytes participate in their respective identities; unsupported target metadata and incomplete closure refuse. B.2 reads old package and artifact evidence at its old strength: a historical `sha256:` recipe or `verified` artifact field does not become a current exact environment lock or output-byte proof. Source/mirror/cache locations remain transport/provenance, not content identity. The accepted B child and [parent reviews](environment-b-central-review.md) leave actual source authentication, `served_by` retention, generation publication and environment realization with later owners.

C.1 carried a strict optional target binding through plan, intent, receipt, native request and inspection; absent binding preserves the legacy state key and record bytes. C.2 added pinned local-root observations and a physical destination lock shared by alias spellings, with separate current-generation and named-child rechecks. C.3 connected one **internal** host-admitted, first-generation local UserProfile `deploy:vibe-bin` apply to the incumbent transaction. Two disposable homes produced separate physical output and bound receipts. Prior intent/receipt, unsupported provider/source, changed body/principal/generation and copied sidecars refuse; a possible write followed by drift retains intent and reports uncertainty. The public shape-only bound entry still refuses; update, remove, interrupted bound recovery, native/plugin/skill effects, offline rootfs and live-system effects are not admitted. See [C.1](environment-c1/central-review.md), [C.2](environment-c2/central-review.md) and [C.3](environment-c3/central-review.md).

## Release and documentation disposition

There is no automatic migration of a user's 1.0.x environment or existing package/artifact/deployment records, no changed legacy bare-install route, and no public bound CLI/MCP command from M-14. The new exported pure types, wire values and internal admitted path do affect the 1.1.0 API/contract documentation. The release notes should say that environment identity and target policy are now distinct, exact lock/generation evidence is stronger only when its inputs are actually captured, and the first bound effect is internal and limited to a local UserProfile `vibe-bin` first install. They must not imply offline Linux, live-system, account/service/mount/boot, native provider or general recovery support.

Four positive A documentation topics are explicitly due at the final documentation gate: `environment-role-source-contract-guidance`, `pure-environment-role-foundation-guidance`, `injected-environment-layout-source-guidance`, and `target-aware-environment-layout-guidance`. The bounded [roles](../environment-roles.md) and [layout](../environment-layout.md) guides explain present meanings but do not prove canonical page/anchor/API/example/build or SDK coverage. B needs exact lock/generation recipe, legacy-record strength, target metadata and source-substitution guidance; C needs the opt-in binding/admission limits, local builtin scope, cooperating-writer warning, postwrite uncertainty and no-recovery limits. The original audit of 58 then-existing permanent `release-impact.json` files found **no implementation subject for at least 33 M-14-B/C product commits**: 13 in B.1/B.2, 19 named in the C.1/C.2/C.3 reviews, plus `59812ce39` (physical fence source law), which their lists omitted. A later [current inventory](environment-bc-subject-inventory-review.md) expands all 33 identities and paths; 71 permanent records now exist and still match 0/33. Those B/C records and separate user/documentation dispositions must be authored and reviewed before this gate can pass; final-documentation topics must then carry every positive disposition. Generated index/citation-only paths may receive an explicit no-user-impact disposition; that does not cancel positive API/source guidance.
